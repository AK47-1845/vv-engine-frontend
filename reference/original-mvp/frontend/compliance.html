Compliance timeline

Regulations and standards this console is built to serve. Dates are static reference, not live clocks.

Why compliance is the forcing function

Safety regulation is what turns verification from a nice to have into a purchased product. Once third party assessment is mandatory, robot makers must buy testing the way drug makers buy clinical trials. Insurers come next: they cannot price policies without failure data. This console produces the artifacts both sides ask for: objective test records, uncertainty numbers, and tamper evident run history.

Upcoming requirements in detail

EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230, Item 24 Verified

What: from 2027-01-20, Notified Body assessment is mandatory for self evolving AI in safety functions (Annex I, Part A, Item 24).

Who it hits: any maker placing adaptive safety related AI into EU machinery.

Evidence it demands: objective conformity records from a competent assessment, with Notified Body queues already reported at 6 to 12 months.

OTA substantial modification rule Verified

What: a safety shifting over the air update counts as a substantial modification, which triggers re-assessment plus a new CE mark.

Who it hits: every vendor that updates robot software after sale, which is everyone.

Evidence it demands: per update test records. Certifying once is not enough. Each release needs fresh evidence, which is exactly what a deterministic re scoring engine plus a run ledger produce.

Frozen weights nuance Reported

What: systems with frozen weights may still self assess, so the mandate bites hardest on learning and self evolving systems plus OTA updated fleets.

Why it matters: it draws the market boundary. Static controllers stay light touch. Adaptive policies and continuously deployed fleets need full verification.

ISO/AWI 25785-1, humanoid safety Reported

What: the humanoid safety standard under TC 299/WG 12, currently an unpublished industrial scope draft, with publication expected late 2026 or 2027.

Why it matters: it will demand purpose built test methods for humanoid kinematics and neural policies. No owner exists yet for the ML native verification layer, which is the gap this console targets.

ISO 13849 and IEC 61508 (in force)

What: the standing safety of machinery and functional safety standards behind safety dossiers.

Evidence they demand: structured safety cases with quantitative backing. The Module 3 dossier compiler aims straight at this output format.

AI Act timeline for embedded systems Reported

What: embedded AI Act obligations phase in toward August 2028.

Why it matters: a second regulatory clock behind the machinery regulation, widening the set of systems that need documented verification.

TIC licensing precedents Reported

What: testing houses are already productizing AI assurance, with programs across AI/TUV NORD, TR/NVIDIA plus Fennec style efforts, and AIQURIS style tooling.

Why it matters: it proves the buyer side is forming, and it sets the partnership path. LTTS can supply the ML native test layer under TIC brands.
