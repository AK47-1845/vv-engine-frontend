Start here: what is this thing?

Short version: this console tests robot brains and hands out report cards.

Longer version: a robot policy is the software that decides how a robot moves. Before that software is allowed near people, products, or expensive machines, somebody has to check that it behaves. This console runs those checks. You pick a policy, press Score, and get back a verdict: TRUSTED (ship it), REVIEW (an engineer must sign off), or BLOCKED (do not deploy). Every metric behind the verdict is shown with its gate, every run is recorded in a tamper evident ledger, and every threshold is labeled DRAFT until it is calibrated on real world data.

This is Module 1 of a bigger verification platform: the trust scorecard engine. Four demo policies are included so you can see all three verdicts within minutes.

The problem in plain words

Why does this exist? Because the robot industry has a testing gap. Over $5B flowed into humanoid and VLA efforts in 18 months [Verified], while industry wide production revenue sits under $20M [Estimated]. That is a 250 to 1 asymmetry between capital in and proven deployment out.

The research behind this console mapped why. World models fall into three schools (pixel renderers, latent JEPA style models, spatial engineered systems), and all of them share five failure modes: drift that compounds until objects pass through walls, an executability gap where perfect looking video commands impossible grasps, silent perceptual hallucination, action marginalization where the model ignores its operator, and the sim to real gap.

The conclusion that built this console: intelligence gets the headlines, verification gets the revenue, and from January 2027 European regulation starts demanding third party assessment. Somebody has to do the checking. See the Compliance page for the regulatory clock.

The 4 policies you can test (working)

Each policy below is a fixed, deterministic test subject. Press a button to score it against the live engine right here. Seed 1, same as the Dashboard.

Clean policy TRUSTED

Follows commands with small sensor noise. This is what a healthy policy looks like. Expect green across the board.

Drifty policy BLOCKED

Accumulates random walk error, so it starts on path and wanders off. Drift and horizon gates catch it. This is the classic long horizon failure.

Playback policy BLOCKED

Ignores every command and replays a fixed tape. The sensitivity probe catches it with a reading of exactly 0.0. A dead steering wheel, found from trajectory data alone.

Synthetic trained policy REVIEW

Stands in for a model trained on synthetic data. Responsive, but noisier, with dropout spikes and a small sim to real bias. Lands in the amber middle: usable, but only with engineer sign off.

How scoring works, step by step

  1. Commands in. The console issues a 200 step reference path as position commands. Same path every time, so policies stay comparable.
  2. Policy executes. The policy under test produces its own 200 step path from those commands.
  3. The probe. The policy runs a second time from the same seed, but with a fixed offset added to the second half of the commands. A policy that obeys controls visibly responds. A playback policy returns the identical path, which is exactly what the sensitivity metric measures.
  4. Spread check. The policy runs five more times from different seeds. The variance across those runs is the uncertainty metric.
  5. Metrics, gates, verdict. Eight metrics are computed (next section), each is graded green, amber, or red against DRAFT thresholds, and the worst gate decides the verdict.

Same policy plus same seed gives byte identical numbers on every machine. That is what makes results auditable.

The 8 metrics in detail

Anything between the green and red cutoffs is amber. Thresholds are DRAFT until Month 1 calibration on Open X-Embodiment pairs.

Verdicts, fail closed

  • TRUSTED. Every gate green. Ship it.
  • REVIEW. At least one amber gate, none red. Needs engineer sign off.
  • BLOCKED. Any red gate. Worst gate wins, always.

Worst gate wins. Seven green gates plus one red gate is BLOCKED. There is no averaging and no partial credit.

Provenance ledger

Every score, compare, and sweep appends one JSONL entry carrying a sequence number, timestamp, payload, previous hash, and entry hash (sha256 over canonical JSON). Verification re hashes the full chain and names the exact sequence number of any break. Try it from the Compliance page evidence panel, or call /api/ledger/verify directly.

Honesty labels

  • DRAFT thresholds. Gates calibrate on Open X-Embodiment pairs in Month 1.
  • Proxies, not proofs. Jerk and bounds rows need a physics sim for real contact dynamics evidence. That is Phase 2.
  • Synthetic fixtures. The four demo policies are synthetic by design. The engine that scores them is the real artifact.
  • Deterministic. Same policy plus same seed gives identical numbers on any machine.

Glossary

  • Policy. The robot software under test. The thing being graded.
  • Rollout. One executed 200 step path from one seed.
  • Seed. The random number key. Same seed gives the same rollout, always.
  • Drift. Distance between the executed path and the commanded path.
  • Horizon. How far along the path the policy stays trustworthy.
  • Sensitivity. How strongly the policy reacts when its commands change.
  • Spread. How much the policy varies between runs. High spread is unpredictability.
  • Frechet distance. A path similarity number. Small means the two paths match closely.
  • Gate. One pass or fail check on one metric.
  • Verdict. The final grade: TRUSTED, REVIEW, or BLOCKED.
  • Ledger. The append only, hash chained record of every run.
  • Probe. The deliberate command offset used to test whether a policy listens.
